# How syntheses are written in Findings (survey, 31 Oct 2026)

Source: findingspress.org/for-authors, Findings Press newsletters, and seven published syntheses. Compiled by a research agent; check details against the live guidelines before submission.

## Guideline facts

- "Syntheses" (literature as the object of study) is a recognised article type. Reviews must answer a question, be replicable and fit the word limit. Opinion pieces are declined.
- Sections: 1. Questions, 2. Methods, 3. Findings. No introduction, road map, literature review section, conclusions, speculation or policy implications.
- 1,000 words main text excluding references. At most 3 figures and 3 tables. Abstract 100 words or fewer, no acronyms. 3 to 8 keywords. Title Case. APA author-date with DOIs. No reference cap (examples 9 to 61, typically 15 to 30).
- SI does not count toward the limit but must not be needed to understand the paper or used to get around the limit.
- Data should be public (guidelines are inconsistent on required vs encouraged).
- One round of single-blind review; judged on soundness, transparency and replicability. US$150 on acceptance (waivers possible). American spelling preferred.
- Editors (Oct 2024 newsletter) want more review articles.

## Template for the boundary-size paper

- Abstract (100 words or fewer): number of schemes and studies, size result by scheme type, duration result.
- 1. Questions (about 150 words): one or two sentences on the gap, then numbered RQs with hypotheses.
- 2. Methods (about 250 to 300 words): inventory source and inclusion rules; search sources, string, date and criteria; counts screened, eligible, included (optionally a PRISMA figure); outcome harmonisation; analysis (rank correlation by scheme type, weighted meta-regression only if N allows); moderators (relative coverage, duration, stringency, scheme type).
- 3. Findings (about 500 to 550 words): one subheading per RQ; short closing summary paragraph with interpretation and caveats (one or two sentences each).
- SI: full database with sources (Zenodo or OSF DOI), search strings, coding rules, polygon sources, sensitivity analyses.

What seems to get syntheses accepted: a sharp question, replicable method with explicit counts (even for rapid reviews), a clear headline result, honest gap or null findings, and open data.
